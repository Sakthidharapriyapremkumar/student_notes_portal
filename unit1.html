UNIT I – Fundamentals of Digital Forensics

Introduction

Definition

Digital Forensics is the branch of forensic science that deals with the identification, collection, preservation, examination, analysis, and presentation of digital evidence obtained from computers, mobile devices, networks, cloud systems, and other digital storage media.Digital forensics ensures that digital evidence is collected without altering its original state so that it can be accepted in a court of law.

Simple Definition

Digital Forensics is the scientific process of finding, collecting, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence to investigate cyber crimes.

Digital Crime Investigation Process

Definition

The Digital Investigation Process is a step-by-step procedure used to identify, collect, preserve, examine, analyze, and present digital evidence to investigate cyber-crimes. Simple Definition Digital Investigation Process is the systematic method of collecting and analyzing digital evidence to solve cyber-crimes. =====================================================================================================================================================

Steps in the Digital Investigation Process

1. Identification

• Identify the incident or cyber-crime.
• Find devices that may contain digital evidence.
• Examples: Computer, mobile phone, USB drive, server.
Example: A company's server is hacked. The investigator identifies the affected server and employee laptops.

2. Collection

• Collect digital evidence carefully.
• Use forensic tools to avoid data modification.
• Document every action performed.
Example: Seize the suspect's laptop and create a forensic image.

3. Preservation

• Protect the original evidence from damage or alteration.
• Maintain the Chain of Custody.
• Store evidence securely.
Example: Seal the hard disk in an evidence bag and calculate its hash value.

4. Examination

• Recover deleted files.
• Extract emails, browser history, call logs, and documents.
• Filter useful information.
Example: Recover deleted financial records from the suspect's computer.

5. Analysis

• Interpret the extracted data.
• Determine how, when, and by whom the crime was committed.
• Correlate evidence from different sources.
Example: Analyze login records to identify the attacker's account.

6. Presentation

• Prepare a clear forensic report.
• Present findings with supporting evidence.
• Submit the report to management or the court.
Example: Present recovered emails and login history as evidence during a trial.

Importance of the Digital Investigation Process

• Ensures evidence is legally admissible.
• Protects evidence from tampering.
• Helps identify criminals.
• Supports cyber-crime investigations.
• Assists in legal proceedings.
Real-Time Example

A bank detects unauthorized online transactions.

• Identification: Find the affected systems.
• Collection: Acquire server logs and customer transaction records.
• Preservation: Create forensic copies and secure the originals.
• Examination: Recover deleted logs and review login records.
• Analysis: Identify the attacker's IP address and account.
• Presentation: Submit the investigation report with supporting evidence.
